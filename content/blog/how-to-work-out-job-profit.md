# How to work out job profit

> **Key facts**
>
> * UK VAT has three rates. 20 percent standard and 5 percent reduced and 0 percent zero rated. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * You must register for VAT when taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months since 1 April 2024. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * CIS deductions are 20 percent where the subcontractor is verified and 30 percent where not and 0 percent where gross payment status is held. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * You can apply to be paid gross under CIS only if you meet HMRC tests on turnover and compliance. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

<p class="tertiary">24 October 2026 · 6 min read · Money</p>

Profit on a job is not the price on the quote. It is what is left after you have paid for everything that job needed. This post shows how to work it out with a worked example that adds up.

No figure below is invented. Each rate was read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026. The links sit at the foot.

## The sum in words

Profit equals income minus costs.

Income is what the customer paid for that job. Costs are what you spent to do that job. Margin equals profit divided by income, times 100.

## A worked example that adds up

A small electrical job. Second fix on a kitchen rewire.

Income paid by the customer: £1,800.00

Costs you paid:

* Materials for this job: £420.00
* Labour paid to a subbie for one day: £280.00
* Skip and parking: £95.00
* Fuel for that job: £35.00

Total costs: £420.00 plus £280.00 plus £95.00 plus £35.00 equals £830.00

Profit: £1,800.00 minus £830.00 equals £970.00

Margin: £970.00 divided by £1,800.00 equals 53.9 percent

Check: £830.00 plus £970.00 equals £1,800.00

That is the cash profit on that job where you did the rest of the work yourself.

## What counts as a cost

Count what you paid because of that job. Materials bought for it. A subbie or labourer paid for it. Skip hire. Plant hire. Parking. Fuel to get there where that fuel is for that job.

Do not put a whole month of phone or insurance into one job. If a cost covers many jobs keep it out of job profit and count it as overhead. Job profit should show whether that price for that job was right.

If you want true profit put in your own time. Value your days at the day rate you need to live on. If you only want cash left after paying others leave your own time out and read the result as cash profit. Know which one you are reading.

## Where VAT sits

VAT is not profit and not a cost of the job in the same sense.

If you are VAT registered and charge VAT, work on net figures. Income net of VAT. Materials net of VAT. The VAT you collect and pay to HMRC sits outside profit.

UK VAT has three rates. 20 percent standard and 5 percent reduced and 0 percent zero rated. Read those rates on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

You must register for VAT when taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months. That level has applied since 1 April 2024. Read that threshold on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

If you are not VAT registered work on gross figures. No adjustment is needed.

## Where CIS sits

If you are a subcontractor and a contractor deducted CIS from your pay that deduction is not a job cost. It is an advance towards your tax and National Insurance that the contractor holds and pays to HMRC.

CIS deductions are 20 percent where the subcontractor is verified and 30 percent where not verified and 0 percent where gross payment status is held. Read those rates on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

The deduction is on labour only. Materials the subcontractor paid for directly sit outside the deduction. VAT on the invoice also sits outside.

Work out profit on income before the CIS deduction. Show CIS as tax held rather than cost. That way the profit reflects the work and the tax record stays correct.

Example where CIS was deducted. Gross for labour £2,000. Materials £400. VAT nil. Total invoice £2,400. Labour liable is £2,000. At 20 percent CIS is £400. Net paid to you is £2,000. Profit is still income £2,400 less costs that include the £400 materials plus other costs. The £400 CIS is not a cost. Keep the £400 on your CIS statement for your return.

## Why small costs matter

Two missed costs turn a good price into a poor day.

Travel and parking are often left out. So is disposal. So is the hour to re visit and touch up.

Put every cost on the job when you pay it. Use the receipt scan in the app or keep a note on the job. The profit you read at the end will then be real and the next quote can be priced from real data rather than a guess.
