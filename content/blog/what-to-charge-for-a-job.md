# What to charge for a trade job

> **Key facts**
>
> * National Living Wage is £12.21 an hour for workers aged 21 and over from April 2025. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * UK VAT has three rates. 20 percent standard and 5 percent reduced and 0 percent zero rated. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * You must register for VAT when taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months since 1 April 2024. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * A business is a contractor for CIS if it pays subcontractors for construction work, or has spent more than £3 million on construction in the last 12 months even if it does not do construction work. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

A price should hold everything the job will take. Time on site and materials and paperwork and a buffer for what you cannot see until you start. This post builds that price step by step with a worked example that adds up.

No figure below is invented. Each rate was read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

## Start with the day rate you need

Your day rate is the number the year needs. Not the number you like.

Take target take home plus business costs for the year. Less what you expect to bill in days. One trader might bill 200 days. Another may bill 160 where weather or school holidays cut the year short. Be honest about that count.

Example. You need £36,000 take home and the business costs are £8,000. Total needed is £44,000. Billable days 200. Day rate needed is £220.00.

Check: £220.00 times 200 equals £44,000

If you employ or pay a labourer their cost sits in the job for that job. Your day rate is still for your time. Price theirs where they work.

National Living Wage is £12.21 an hour for workers aged 21 and over from April 2025. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026. That is the floor not the price.

## Build the job price

Add the parts that make the job whole.

### Labour

Days on site at your day rate. Include the time to set up and clear up. Include a second visit where the job needs one.

Use the day rate you worked out above. Not a guess at what the job might bear.

### Materials

What you will buy for this job at cost to you plus the time to get it. Do not add profit to materials by hiding it in that line. Profit belongs in the total. Materials are at cost inside the build.

### Paperwork

The hour to price and write the quote. The hour to do the invoice and chase. The photos and notes on the job. Many traders leave this out and then fund it from their evening.

Add a paperwork figure. £60 is common for quoting and invoicing a small job. More where plans or calls or site visits are needed before you can price.

### Contingency

Every job has a risk you cannot see from the call. Ground that is harder than it looked. Access that needs a longer run. A second fix that takes half a day more.

Hold a buffer inside the total. Five percent is common for straight work. More for roofs and ground work and old buildings. The customer does not need to see this line. The customer needs a price that holds.

## A worked example that adds up

Bathroom refit. Three days on site.

Days: 3 times £220.00 equals £660.00

Materials for the job: £580.00

Paperwork and admin: £70.00

Subtotal before buffer: £660.00 plus £580.00 plus £70.00 equals £1,310.00

Contingency at 5 percent: £1,310.00 times 5 percent equals £65.50

Price before VAT: £1,310.00 plus £65.50 equals £1,375.50

Check: £660.00 plus £580.00 plus £70.00 plus £65.50 equals £1,375.50

## Where VAT goes

VAT is not part of the build above. It sits on the invoice as the law says.

UK VAT has three rates. 20 percent standard and 5 percent reduced and 0 percent zero rated. Read those rates on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

If you are VAT registered you charge VAT at the right rate and show it as a separate line. £1,375.50 at 20 percent is £275.10. Total to pay is £1,650.60.

Check: £1,375.50 plus £275.10 equals £1,650.60

You must register when taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months. That level has applied since 1 April 2024. Read that threshold on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

If you are not VAT registered do not charge VAT. The £1,375.50 is what the customer pays.

## Where CIS goes

CIS is not a price build item.

A business is a contractor for CIS if it pays subcontractors for construction work, or has spent more than £3 million on construction in the last 12 months even if it does not do construction. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

If you invoice a contractor and CIS applies, the deduction is an advance towards your tax. It does not change the price. The price is what was agreed. The contractor holds the CIS deduction and pays you the balance.

If you pay another trade for construction work you may need to verify them and deduct at the rate HMRC gives. That deduction is not a cost saving to you. It is tax held for them.

## Show the customer the work not the build

The build above is for your eyes. The quote the customer sees should show work lines they can picture. Supply and fit and testing and waste. A note for access or making good where that matters.

Keep contingency inside the total. Keep the day rate workings behind the quote. The customer chooses the value of the finished work. Your workings are yours.
