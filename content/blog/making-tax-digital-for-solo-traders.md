# Making Tax Digital for solo traders

> **Key facts**
>
> * You need to use MTD for Income Tax from 6 April 2026 if qualifying income in 2024/25 was over £50,000. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * You need to use it from 6 April 2027 if qualifying income in 2025/25 was over £30,000. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * You need to use it from 6 April 2028 if qualifying income in 2026/27 was over £20,000. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * Each tax year has four updates due 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May, then the tax return. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

<p class="tertiary">By the YellowTradie team. Sources linked throughout.</p>

<p class="tertiary">24 October 2026 · 7 min read · Tax</p>

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax changes how some sole traders tell HMRC about profit. It does not remove the Self Assessment tax return. It adds four short updates during the year before the return.

This post is for a trader on his own. The dates and lines below were read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026. Links sit at the foot.

## Who it applies to

You need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax if you meet all of these.

* You are a sole trader registered for Self Assessment or a landlord
* You get income from self employment or property or both
* Your qualifying income for the test year is over the line for that year

Qualifying income is total income before expenses from self employment and property taken together. HMRC works it out from the Self Assessment return you sent in the previous year.

Landlords have their own count inside the same test. Partnerships will need to use it in future. HMRC will set that date later.

If you are under the line you keep sending Self Assessment as before.

## The staged lines

The line moves down in steps. Three steps are fixed now.

* Over £50,000 in 2024/25 means you should have started from 6 April 2026 for the year 2026/27
* Over £30,000 in 2025/26 means you need to use it from 6 April 2027
* Over £20,000 in 2026/27 means you need to use it from 6 April 2028

Check the gross figure on the return HMRC used. A trader on £55,000 taken before costs is over the £50,000 line even if profit after materials and fuel is lower.

To sign up you must be registered for Self Assessment and have sent a return in the last two years. HMRC has said from September 2026 it will start to sign up those who need to use it for 2026/27 and have not signed themselves up. If HMRC signs you up it uses the information it already holds. If your details have changed you must update them.

Where you are over the line you need software that can keep digital records and send the updates and the return. YellowTradie keeps records in HMRC quarters and shows money received since 6 April. It does not yet file an update with HMRC. That is stated on the Making Tax Digital feature page.

## The four update dates

The four standard update periods and the date each one must be sent by are fixed by HMRC. Each update covers from 6 April to the end of the period. The figures are cumulative through the year so the last holds the whole year.

* 6 April to 5 July 2026. Send by 7 August 2026
* 6 April to 5 October 2026. Send by 7 November 2026
* 6 April to 5 January 2027. Send by 7 February 2027
* 6 April to 5 April 2027. Send by 7 May 2027

After the fourth update you also complete the tax return for the year through the same software.

If you need to use it from April 2026 HMRC has said it will not apply penalty points for late quarterly updates in the first year 2026/27. Penalties still apply for a late tax return or for paying late.

## A worked example that adds up

You are a sole trader in the £50,000 line. You started from 6 April 2026.

You record income and costs digitally. At the end of each period you look at what you have been paid since 6 April rather than in that quarter alone.

Example for 2026/27 where figures are net of VAT.

* By 5 July you have been paid £8,200 since 6 April. That is what the first update covers
* By 5 October you have been paid £19,500 since 6 April. That is what the second covers
* By 5 January you have been paid £31,400 since 6 April. That is what the third covers
* By 5 April you have been paid £42,000 since 6 April. That is what the fourth covers

Check: £8,200 plus £11,300 plus £11,900 plus £10,600 equals £42,000

The four updates show how the year builds. If you find an early record was wrong you fix the record and the next cumulative update carries the fix. You do not send a separate correction for the past quarter alone.

Your costs sit in the same record. At the return you bring income and expenses together with allowances and CIS already deducted so the bill is right.

## What counts as a miss

HMRC uses a points model for regular submissions. You get a point each time you miss a deadline. HMRC tells you about each point. At a set number of points a £200 penalty is charged and you are told. Every later miss after the threshold also costs £200.

For quarterly updates including Making Tax Digital for Income Tax the threshold is four points. The period of compliance to reset points at the threshold is twelve months. The time limit for HMRC to levy a point for a quarterly miss is eleven weeks. The time limit to assess a penalty is two years.

These rules are in the penalties for late submission publication on gov.uk read on 24 October 2026. The publication sets 2 points for annual submissions and 4 for quarterly and 5 for monthly.

Points that are not at the threshold expire after two years.

## Four things trades get wrong

### One. Turnover and profit

The line is qualifying income before expenses. A trader on £52,000 gross who spends £20,000 on materials is still over £50,000. Check gross rather than profit.

### Two. Self Assessment has gone

It has not. You send a Self Assessment return for the year before you start. Once you are in Making Tax Digital you still send a tax return through software after the four updates.

### Three. Each quarter stands alone

It does not. Each update covers from 6 April to the end of that period. The second holds the first. The fourth holds the whole year.

### Four. VAT and CIS sit inside without care

If you are VAT registered the figure to count against the line is money received net of VAT. CIS deductions are advance tax not sales. Keep the three apart so the update does not overstate takings. The app shows VAT where you are VAT registered and keeps CIS as a separate record for that reason.

## What to do this week

1. Check if you are in it. Use the gov.uk tool. It asks about 2024/25 and 2025/26 and the income you declared or will declare. Over £50,000 in 2024/25 means 2026/27. Over £30,000 in 2025/26 means April 2027.

2. Check your Self Assessment login. You need the same user ID and password you used to register. If you have not sent a return in the last two years send one first. An agent can sign you up.

3. Pick software that works with Making Tax Digital. It must keep digital records and send the four updates and the return.

4. Sign up if you are due for 2026/27. If HMRC has not signed you up use the online service. You must confirm business name and address and trade and start date if within the last two years and which year you start.

5. Keep records from 6 April in one place. Record income and expenses digitally with the thirteen expense categories HMRC uses for the update.
