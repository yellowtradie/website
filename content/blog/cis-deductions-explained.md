# CIS deductions explained

> **Key facts**
>
> * CIS deduction rates are 20 percent for registered subcontractors and 30 percent for unregistered and 0 percent where gross payment status is held. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * There is no £1,000 CIS threshold. A business is a contractor if it pays subcontractors for construction work, or has spent more than £3 million on construction in the last 12 months. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * CIS is on labour only. Materials the subcontractor paid for directly and VAT on the invoice sit outside the deduction. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * Every contractor must give the subcontractor a payment and deduction statement within 14 days of the end of the tax month, which runs from the 6th to the 5th. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

<p class="tertiary">24 October 2026 · 7 min read · CIS</p>

The Construction Industry Scheme sets how a contractor pays a subcontractor. Where it applies the contractor holds back part of the payment and sends it to HMRC. That held amount counts towards the subcontractor’s tax. This post sets out the three rates and what is inside and outside the deduction with a worked example that adds up.

No figure below is invented. Each rule was read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

## The three rates

CIS deduction rates are 20 percent for registered subcontractors and 30 percent for unregistered and 0 percent where gross payment status is held. Read those rates on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

HMRC tells the contractor which rate to use when the contractor verifies the subcontractor. The deduction is an advance towards the subcontractor’s Income Tax and National Insurance. The contractor must pay it to HMRC.

The contractor verifies the subcontractor before paying. The rate given is the rate used for that payment. If the subcontractor later gains gross payment status the rate changes for later payments rather than old ones.

## The £1,000 question

There is no £1,000 CIS threshold. Some traders expect a small job to be outside CIS because it is under £1,000. That is not how the test works.

A business is a contractor for CIS if it pays subcontractors for construction work, or if it does not do construction work but has spent more than £3 million on construction in the last 12 months. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

If you are a contractor and the work is construction operations and the customer is a subcontractor the scheme can apply even on a small sum. The amount does not put it outside.

## Private householders

Private householders are not contractors for CIS.

Work you do for a homeowner in their own home is outside CIS. That is why a small domestic job often has no CIS. It is not a threshold. It is a status. The householder is not a contractor.

If your client is a contractor and you are a subcontractor doing work on a householder’s home for that contractor the payment between you can still be inside CIS. The end customer does not decide the status between the two trades. Read that line between householders and contractors in the CIS guide linked at the foot.

## Labour only

CIS is on labour only.

Materials the subcontractor paid for directly sit outside the deduction. VAT on the invoice also sits outside. Fuel used other than travel and plant hire and manufacturing or prefabricating materials sit outside where the subcontractor paid for them directly and can evidence them. Read that list on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

You cannot take off a guess for materials where none were supplied. A labour only job is wholly liable. Labour only at £1,000 with no materials has £1,000 liable to the deduction.

Where materials are supplied the contractor deducts their cost from the gross before working out CIS. The contractor can ask for evidence such as receipts. If the subcontractor cannot show it the contractor estimates the cost.

## How the deduction is worked out

Start with the gross amount for the work. Take off VAT shown on the invoice. Take off the direct cost of materials the subcontractor paid for and can evidence. What is left is the amount liable. Apply the rate HMRC gave for that subcontractor.

## A worked example that adds up

A subcontractor invoices a contractor for wall tiling. Supply and fix. VAT nil.

Gross for the job: £1,000

Materials the subcontractor paid for: £200

Labour liable: £800

At 20 percent the deduction is £160. Net paid to the subcontractor is £840.

Check: £1,000 minus £160 equals £840. £800 times 20 percent equals £160

At 30 percent the deduction is £240. Net paid is £760.

Check: £1,000 minus £240 equals £760. £800 times 30 percent equals £240

At 0 percent where gross payment status is held there is no deduction. Net paid is £1,000.

The contractor must pay the deduction to HMRC. The subcontractor keeps the payment and deduction statement for the tax return.

### The contractor’s return for that job

For the £1,000 example at 20 percent the contractor enters on the monthly return:

Total payment £1,000. Direct cost of materials £200. Amount deducted £160.

That matches the CIS 340 example D1 style where labour only at £200 with 20 percent gives £40 deducted and £160 paid.

## The statement and the tax month

Every contractor must give the subcontractor a payment and deduction statement within 14 days of the end of the tax month. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

The CIS tax month runs from the 6th of one month to the 5th of the next. So 6 May to 5 June is one tax month. The statement for that month is due by 19 June. The same rule moves each month.

Keep every statement. At Self Assessment the CIS deducted is an advance towards the bill. It reduces what you still need to pay. The return shows profit from the year and the statements show tax already held.

## Small traps

One. Thinking CIS is VAT. It is not VAT. CIS is held from labour and sent to HMRC as tax. VAT is charged on sales and reclaimed on purchases. The two sit on the same invoice but are worked in separate boxes.

Two. Applying CIS to a householder invoice. Householder work is outside CIS. Do not hold CIS from a payment a householder makes to you. Do not expect a householder to verify you.

Three. Forgetting the statement. The deduction without a statement leaves the subcontractor with no proof at year end. Give it within 14 days as the rule says.

Four. Mixing gross and net when you work out profit. Count income before CIS when you work out job profit. CIS held is tax already paid not a job cost. Count it on the tax record rather than as cost.

## What to do next

If you are a subcontractor check your verification status with HMRC. If you are verified your deduction is 20 percent. If you are not yet registered it is 30 percent. Gross payment status needs a separate HMRC test.

If you are a contractor verify each subcontractor before the first payment. Use the rate HMRC gives. Pay the deduction to HMRC and give the statement within 14 days of the month end.

If you do both roles keep the two piles apart. Income where you are the subcontractor and payments where you are the contractor go on separate returns.
