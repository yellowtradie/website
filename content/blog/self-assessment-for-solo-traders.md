# Self Assessment for solo traders

> **Key facts**
>
> * Paper return for 2025/26 is due 31 October 2026. Online return is due 31 January 2027. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * Balancing payment and first payment on account for 2025/26 are due 31 January 2027. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * Second payment on account is due 31 July 2027 where payments on account apply. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * Late filing penalty starts at £100. After three months HMRC can charge £10 a day up to £900. After six and twelve months further 5 percent penalties can apply. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

<p class="tertiary">By the YellowTradie team. Sources linked throughout.</p>

<p class="tertiary">24 October 2026 · 8 min read · Tax</p>

Self Assessment is how a sole trader tells HMRC what was earned and what is owed. It ends with two dates that land together. The filing date and the payment date.

This post sets out the dates for 2025/26 and what happens if you miss them. Every date and penalty below was read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

## The dates to put in your diary

For the year 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026 the dates are:

* 5 October 2026. Tell HMRC you need to send a return if you have not sent one before. You do this by registering for Self Assessment.
* 31 October 2026 at 11.59pm. Paper return for 2025/26 must reach HMRC.
* 31 January 2027 at 11.59pm. Online return for 2025/26 must reach HMRC. You can send from 6 April 2026 onwards.
* 31 January 2027. Balancing payment for 2025/26 and first payment on account towards 2026/27 where you make payments on account.
* 31 July 2027. Second payment on account towards 2026/27 where payments on account apply.

If you want HMRC to collect through your tax code you must send by 30 December 2026 rather than 31 January.

If a deadline falls on a weekend or a bank holiday the date does not move. For payments the money must reach HMRC on time. Bacs and Direct Debit need to allow for working days.

## What a payment on account is

Payments on account apply when your last Self Assessment bill was over £1,000 and you paid less than 80 percent of what was due through PAYE or tax code.

Each payment on account is half of the previous year bill. Two equal payments. One with the balancing payment on 31 January. One on 31 July.

At the next 31 January HMRC compares what you have paid on account with what you actually owe for that year. You pay the balance if you still owe. You get a refund if you paid too much.

A trader whose bill is under £1,000 does not make payments on account.

## A worked example that adds up

Take the year 2025/26 where you have profit of £32,000 and no other income and no CIS deducted.

Income tax on £32,000 using 2025/26 bands for England Wales and Northern Ireland:

* Personal allowance £12,570 at 0 percent is £0
* £12,571 to £32,000 is £19,430 at 20 percent is £3,886.00

Class 4 National Insurance at 6 percent between £12,570 and £50,270:

* £19,430 at 6 percent is £1,165.80

Bill for 2025/26 is £3,886.00 plus £1,165.80 equals £5,051.80

Check: £3,886.00 plus £1,165.80 equals £5,051.80

Payments on account towards 2026/27:

* First payment on account 31 January 2027 is half of £5,051.80 equals £2,525.90
* Second payment on account 31 July 2027 is £2,525.90

On 31 January 2027 you pay balancing payment £5,051.80 plus first payment on account £2,525.90 equals £7,577.70

Check: £5,051.80 plus £2,525.90 equals £7,577.70

On 31 July 2027 you pay second payment on account £2,525.90

The app shows the same split where you have other income and CIS as a credit. The estimate carries the line an estimate from your records not advice.

## What late filing costs

This scale is on the Self Assessment penalties page on gov.uk read on 24 October 2026.

* One day late. £100 fixed penalty
* Three months late. £10 a day up to £900 in addition to the £100. That is 90 days at £10
* Six months late. A further 5 percent of tax due or £300 whichever is higher
* Twelve months late. Another 5 percent of tax due or £300 whichever is higher

Example. Tax due £5,051.80. Filed six months late.

* £100 fixed
* £10 a day for 90 days is £900
* 5 percent of £5,051.80 is £252.59 so £300 applies

Total filing penalties at six months is £100 plus £900 plus £300 equals £1,300.00

Check: £100 plus £900 equals £1,000. £1,000 plus £300 equals £1,300

At twelve months another 5 percent or £300 whichever is higher is added.

These penalties apply to filing even where no tax is owed or where tax has been paid late. Interest is charged on what is still owed.

## What late payment costs

This scale is on the pay your Self Assessment bill page and the penalties page on gov.uk read on 24 October 2026.

* 30 days late. 5 percent of tax unpaid at that date
* Six months late. Another 5 percent of tax unpaid at that date
* Twelve months late. Another 5 percent of tax unpaid at that date

Interest is charged on what is owed on top of the penalties. HMRC charges interest from the due date until the date you pay.

Example. Balancing payment £5,051.80 paid 30 days late.

* 5 percent of £5,051.80 equals £252.59

Check: £5,051.80 times 5 percent equals £252.59

If still unpaid at six months another 5 percent can be added on the amount unpaid at that date.

If HMRC must estimate your bill because you still have not sent a return the estimate can be higher than what you owe. Send the return so the real figure replaces it.

## Making Tax Digital and these dates

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax does not replace the 31 January filing date. It adds four short updates during the year before the return.

For 2026/27 the four updates and deadlines are:

* 6 April to 5 July 2026 by 7 August 2026
* 6 April to 5 October 2026 by 7 November 2026
* 6 April to 5 January 2027 by 7 February 2027
* 6 April to 5 April 2027 by 7 May 2027

Each update is cumulative from 6 April. The return after the fourth still uses 31 January as its filing and payment date.

## What to do this week

1. Check if you need to tell HMRC by 5 October 2026. If you have never sent a return register now.

2. Decide paper or online. Paper reaches HMRC by 31 October 2026. Online by 31 January 2027. An online return can be sent any time from 6 April 2026.

3. Note payment on 31 January 2027. That is the balancing payment plus first payment on account where you make them. Put both figures in your diary.

4. Note second payment on account 31 July 2027 if you make payments on account.

5. Keep proof of posting and a copy of what you sent. Keep the Self Assessment reference and the payment reference so you can match what you paid.
