# How to raise a VAT invoice

> **Key facts**
>
> * A VAT invoice must show a unique invoice number and your VAT number and the invoice date and the date of supply. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * A VAT invoice must show the VAT rate and VAT amount per item and the total without VAT and total VAT and total with VAT. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * You must issue a VAT invoice within 30 days of supply where you are VAT registered. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * UK VAT has three rates. 20 percent standard and 5 percent reduced and 0 percent zero rated. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

<p class="tertiary">By the YellowTradie team. Sources linked throughout.</p>

<p class="tertiary">24 October 2026 · 7 min read · Invoicing</p>

A VAT invoice is a legal record. HMRC sets what must be on it and when you must issue it. A quote or an estimate is not one. A plain invoice from a business that is not VAT registered is not one either.

This post sets out what an invoice must show taken from HMRC and gov.uk read on 24 October 2026. It ends with a full worked VAT invoice that adds up.

## When you need a VAT invoice

You must issue a VAT invoice when you are VAT registered and you make a supply of goods or services that is subject to VAT to a customer who is in business or where VAT is charged.

You must register when taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months. That level has applied since 1 April 2024. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

UK VAT has three rates. 20 percent standard and 5 percent reduced and 0 percent zero rated. Read those rates on gov.uk on 24 October 2026. Zero rated is taxable at 0 percent. Exempt is not taxable.

The app shows VAT only when you have a VAT number in Settings and the VAT amount is above zero. Where you are not VAT registered the invoice prints without a VAT row.

## What an invoice must show from HMRC

This list is from gov.uk on VAT record keeping and VAT invoices read on 24 October 2026.

Every VAT invoice must show:

* A unique invoice number that follows on from the last one you issued. No gaps and no reuse.
* Your business name and address
* Your VAT number
* The customer name and address
* The invoice date
* The date of supply also called the tax point. This is when the work was done or goods supplied. It can be the same as the invoice date where you invoice on completion.
* A description of the goods or services. Enough for the customer and for HMRC to know what was supplied.
* Quantity and unit price for each line
* VAT rate applied to each item where more than one rate applies
* VAT amount per item
* Total without VAT
* Total VAT
* Total with VAT
* Payment terms and how to pay. Use the invoice number as the reference so the bank payment can be matched.
* Bank details with account name and sort code and account number

Where you charge VAT at more than one rate show each rate and its VAT in its own row.

You must issue the VAT invoice within 30 days of the supply.

## A worked VAT invoice that adds up

An electrical job for a domestic customer who hired you direct. No CIS on this one. VAT at 20 percent.

Business: Northfield Electrical (example trader), 12 Station Road, Bristol BS1 2AW. VAT no 123 4567 89. Tel 07700 900123. hello@example-trader.co.uk

Customer: Example Homeowner, 8 Elmwood Road, Clifton, Bristol BS8 1AP

Invoice number: INV-2042. Invoice date 18 November 2026. Date of supply 18 November 2026. Due date 2 December 2026.

Lines:

* Labour first fix 2 days at £280.00 a day equals £560.00. VAT at 20 percent
* Labour second fix 1 day at £280.00 equals £280.00. VAT at 20 percent
* Materials consumer unit and fittings 1 at £420.00 equals £420.00. VAT at 20 percent

Check on lines: £560.00 plus £280.00 plus £420.00 equals £1,260.00

Totals:

* Subtotal without VAT is £1,260.00
* VAT at 20 percent is £1,260.00 times 20 percent equals £252.00
* Total with VAT is £1,260.00 plus £252.00 equals £1,512.00
* Paid to date is £0.00
* Balance due is £1,512.00

Check: £1,260.00 plus £252.00 equals £1,512.00

Payment: Use INV-2042 as reference. Sort code 04-00-75 account 12345678. Payment due by 2 December 2026. Late payment may carry interest under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 where it applies to business customers.

That set of lines and totals shows every field HMRC asks for. A copy of what you sent should be kept for your VAT records.

## Where CIS sits on the same page

Some invoices carry both VAT and a CIS deduction. CIS is held from labour only and sent to HMRC as tax. VAT is charged on sales and reclaimed on purchases. The two are worked in separate boxes.

Take the VAT example above where the invoice was to a CIS contractor.

Labour subject to CIS is £840.00

Materials excluded from CIS is £420.00

CIS at 20 percent on £840.00 is £168.00

Amount payable after CIS is £1,512.00 minus £168.00 equals £1,344.00

Check: £840.00 times 20 percent equals £168.00. £1,512.00 minus £168.00 equals £1,344.00

VAT £252.00 stays on the full subtotal and is not deducted under CIS.

The CIS block shows labour and materials as separate figures, the rate, the amount deducted, and the month that deduction belongs to from the 6th to the 5th. The contractor pays the £168.00 to HMRC and gives the subcontractor a statement within 14 days of the end of that tax month.

The invoice template page shows the full CIS block at 20 percent on labour £1,400 giving £280 deducted with materials £600 outside and payable after CIS £2,120.

## Common mistakes

One. Reusing a number. HMRC requires unique numbers that follow on. A gap makes your records harder to defend.

Two. Omitting the VAT number. A VAT invoice without it is not a VAT invoice.

Three. Showing only the VAT inclusive total with no breakdown. You must show total without VAT and total VAT as separate figures.

Four. Charging VAT when you are not registered. Do not add VAT unless you hold a VAT number. The £90,000 threshold does not let you charge VAT early.

Five. Forgetting the date of supply. Where the job spans days put the date the work was completed.

Six. Using the headline invoice total where CIS applies rather than the amount payable after CIS. The customer pays the payable figure and the contractor accounts for the deduction.

## How to use the app for this

The app keeps the same fields. Invoice number is unique and sequential. Name and address come from Settings and the customer record. Job title shows on the invoice so the customer can match the paper to the work.

Each line carries description and quantity and unit price and amount. VAT rate and VAT amount show per line and in totals where you are VAT registered.

The invoice number is the payment reference. The bank block prints under the total with Reference INV-2042 as in the example. The customer puts that reference on the bank transfer.

Where you are a subcontractor to a CIS contractor the invoice carries the CIS block with labour and materials shown apart at the rate held. That snapshot is written when you raise the invoice and does not change if you later edit the customer.
