# How to chase unpaid invoices

> **Key facts**
>
> * Statutory interest on a late business debt is 8 percent a year above the Bank of England base rate. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * You can claim a fixed sum for recovery costs. £40 on debts up to £999.99. £70 on debts of £1,000 to £9,999.99. £100 on debts of £10,000 or more. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * Bank Rate was 4.00 percent on 24 October 2026 on bankofengland.co.uk. Statutory rate on that date was 12.00 percent a year.
> * If the debtor is an individual you must give 30 days to reply to a letter before action under the Pre-action Protocol for Debt Claims. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

<p class="tertiary">24 October 2026 · 8 min read · Invoicing</p>

Late payment is normal in trade work. Domestic customers forget. Small builders wait for their own customer to pay. The law gives you a clear ladder to follow. This post sets it out in four steps. Each step has the words to use. The interest rules sit after the letters. The note on when it becomes a letter before action sits at the end.

This is general information. It is not legal advice. Take advice where the facts are disputed or the sums are large.

## When interest starts

Interest starts when payment becomes late. That is the day after the agreed payment date. If your terms say 14 days, the 15th day is the first late day. If no credit period was agreed in a business to business contract, the Act gives a default of 30 days to pay. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

Interest is simple and runs per day. Daily rate equals annual rate divided by 365. Interest due equals daily rate times the amount still owed times days overdue. Use the balance that is late. Not the original invoice total.

Fixed sums are separate from interest. You can claim only one fixed sum per debt when payment is late. You can also claim reasonable recovery costs beyond the fixed sum where that fixed sum does not cover your costs. You will need to show them.

## The four step ladder

The app uses the same four steps. The invoice number is the payment reference on every chase. The amount shown is the amount still owed. So the customer can pay from that one message.

### Step one. Friendly reminder on the due date

Send this on the day the invoice falls due. No warning of interest yet. Keep it short. Include the reference and the bank details.

> Subject: Payment reminder: INV-2040 due 19 November 2026
>
> Dear Example Client,
>
> Invoice INV-2040 for £1,134.00 is due today. You paid £200.00. Balance now due is £934.00. Due date was 19 November 2026.
>
> Please pay £934.00 by bank transfer. Use INV-2040 as the reference.
>
> Account: Northfield Electrical (example trader). Sort code 04-00-75. Account 12345678. Reference INV-2040.
>
> If you paid in the last day please ignore this. If there is a problem with the work please reply here.
>
> Kind regards,
> J. Lee (example trader). Northfield Electrical

Check. £1,134.00 minus £200.00 equals £934.00. No interest is added at this stage.

### Step two. Firm reminder at 7 days overdue

Send at about 7 days overdue. Name the due date. Name the days overdue. Set a new date to pay within 7 days. Still no interest added at this stage. Name the right to interest so the customer knows it exists.

> Subject: Overdue: INV-2040. £934.00. 7 days overdue
>
> Dear Example Client,
>
> Invoice INV-2040 for £1,134.00 was due on 19 November 2026. We received £200.00. Balance of £934.00 is now 7 days overdue.
>
> Please pay £934.00 by 3 December 2026. Reference INV-2040. Sort code 04-00-75. Account 12345678.
>
> Our terms are 14 days. Late payment by a business may carry interest under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998. Nothing is added on this letter.
>
> If you cannot pay in full please write to propose a payment plan. If you dispute the invoice please write why.
>
> Kind regards,
> J. Lee. Northfield Electrical

### Step three. Notice with interest and fixed sum

Send after about 14 days overdue. Show the maths. Add interest for the days since the due date. Add the fixed sum. Show the daily rate that continues.

Example maths on 10 December 2026 where the balance is £934.00 and payment was due 19 November 2026. That is 21 days late. Annual statutory rate on 24 October 2026 was 12.00 percent where base rate was 4.00 percent.

Daily rate is £934.00 times 12.00 percent divided by 365. That is £0.31 a day. For 21 days that is £6.45. Fixed sum where debt is under £1,000 is £40.00.

Total now due is £934.00 plus £6.45 plus £40.00. That equals £980.45.

> Subject: Formal notice: INV-2040 overdue with interest and fixed sum
>
> Dear Example Client,
>
> Invoice INV-2040 was due on 19 November 2026. Balance of £934.00 is 21 days overdue. This letter claims interest and the fixed sum the law allows for business debts.
>
> Balance £934.00
> Interest for 21 days at 12.00 percent a year (8 percent plus base rate 4.00 percent on 24 October 2026) £6.45
> Fixed sum under £1,000 £40.00
> Total now due £980.45
>
> Daily rate is £0.31 a day. Interest continues until paid. That rate is simple interest only. It is 12.00 percent divided by 365 times the balance.
>
> Please pay £980.45 by 17 December 2026. Reference INV-2040. Sort code 04-00-75. Account 12345678.
>
> If you dispute this please write why and send any documents.
>
> Kind regards,
> J. Lee. Northfield Electrical

Check. £934.00 plus £6.45 plus £40.00 equals £980.45. £934.00 times 12 percent equals £112.08 a year. Divided by 365 equals £0.31 a day. Times 21 equals £6.45.

### Step four. Letter before action

This is the formal letter the court expects to see. The Pre-action Protocol for Debt Claims applies where a business claims from an individual. It requires a letter that sets out the claim and gives 30 days to respond. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026. Where the debtor is a business the Practice Direction on Pre-Action Conduct still requires reasonable time to respond. In practice give 30 days.

You must enclose the invoice and the terms and the protocol information sheet and the reply form. List what is enclosed in the letter.

> LETTER BEFORE ACTION - DO NOT IGNORE
>
> Northfield Electrical (example trader)
> 12 Station Road, Bristol BS1 2AW. 07700 900123. hello@example-trader.co.uk
> 18 December 2026
>
> Example Client (illustrative only)
> 8 Elmwood Road, Clifton, Bristol BS8 1AP
>
> Claim for INV-2040
>
> Dear Example Client,
>
> Invoice INV-2040 was issued on 5 November 2026. Payment was due on 19 November 2026. Total was £1,134.00. We received £200.00. Balance is £934.00. With interest and fixed sum to 10 December 2026 the total was £980.45. Daily interest of £0.31 continues.
>
> You must act within 30 days by 17 January 2027
>
> 1. Pay £980.45 plus further daily interest to 04-00-75 12345678 Reference INV-2040.
> 2. Dispute in writing using the enclosed reply form and send documents.
> 3. Propose a payment plan with dates and amounts.
>
> Enclosed: INV-2040 and terms and information sheet and reply form as the Protocol requires.
>
> If we do not hear from you we will start a County Court claim for debt and interest and costs. The court expects you to have had a chance to respond. It will also expect you to have considered mediation.
>
> Yours faithfully,
> J. Lee (example trader). Northfield Electrical
> Signed: __________________ Date: 18 December 2026

This letter carries a deadline. Do not send it without the enclosures. Keep proof of postage and a copy of what you sent.

## Interest rules in one place

Statutory interest is 8 percent above the Bank of England base rate a year. You do not need to have written that rate into your terms to claim it for business to business debts. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

You can instead set your own contract rate in your written terms. That contract rate applies where the customer agreed it. The sum is worked the same way with your figure in place of statutory. The contract rate must be fair where the customer is a business and must not create a penalty in English law.

Daily rate equals annual rate divided by 365 times balance. Example above is £0.31 a day for a £934.00 balance at 12.00 percent.

Fixed sums are set by law. Up to £999.99 debt is £40. £1,000 to £9,999.99 is £70. £10,000 and over is £100. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026. Only one fixed sum per debt on first lateness. You can claim reasonable costs beyond that only where you can show them.

If the base rate moves mid debt split the debt by the dates of each rate change. Work each period on the base rate that applied in that period. Interest stops when the debt is paid.

If only part of the invoice is late use the unpaid amount. Not the headline total.

## The customer who is not a business

The Act covers business to business supply. A consumer is a person acting outside their trade for personal use. A couple who hire you for their own home are consumers. A landlord who owns property as a business is a business customer for this purpose.

For a consumer the Act does not give you statutory interest and fixed sums in the same way. Interest on a late payment from a consumer depends on your contract terms and on what is fair under consumer law. A term that charges interest to a consumer must meet the fairness test in the Consumer Rights Act 2015. It must be prominent and balanced. It must not punish out of proportion to your loss.

In practice with a consumer in their own home name a clear due date and a modest annual rate. Bring it to the customer before the work starts. Put it on the invoice so they can see it. Do not add the business fixed sums of £40 and £70 and £100 to a consumer bill and present them as law. They are not.

A court can award interest of 8 percent under section 69 of the County Courts Act 1984 where the 1998 Act does not apply. Read make a court claim for money on gov.uk on 24 October 2026. Claims to £10,000 in England and Wales go to the small claims track.

## Small claims note

If payment still does not come start a claim for money to the County Court. Read make a court claim for money on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

Track. Claims to £10,000 in England and Wales go to the small claims track. Interest is 8 percent under County Courts Act 1984 section 69 where the 1998 Act does not apply. The court fee depends on the sum claimed. Check the fee on gov.uk at the date you claim.

Before you claim keep copies of all letters and proof of postage. You must have sent a letter before action and given time to respond. The court will expect that.
