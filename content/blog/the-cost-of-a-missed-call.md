# The cost of a missed call

> **Key facts**
>
> * 1 missed call a week at £250 a job is £13,000 a year at 52 weeks. 3 a week is £39,000. Read the calculator on 24 October 2026.
> * 1 missed call a week at £500 is £26,000 a year. 3 a week is £78,000. Read the calculator on 24 October 2026.
> * At a 50 percent conversion rate where half of callers would have booked, halve each figure. So 3 a week at £250 is £19,500 a year. Read the calculator on 24 October 2026.
> * UK VAT has three rates. 20 percent standard and 5 percent reduced and 0 percent zero rated. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

A missed call is not a lost message. It is a job that went to someone who picked up.

This post puts a price on it using your own numbers. No figure for how many calls a trader misses is assumed. You type the count and the value and the post shows the arithmetic that links them.

## The arithmetic of one lost job

Start with what one job from that call would have been worth to you. Not turnover across the year. The value of that one job.

Example. A small electrical job priced at £250. Or a bathroom repair at £500. Or a day of labour at £280. Use a typical value from those calls.

Yearly cost equals missed calls in a week times value of a job times 52.

In symbols: yearly equals missed per week times job value times 52. Where not every caller would have booked, yearly equals missed per week times job value times 52 times conversion rate.

The calculator that does this for you is the [missed call cost calculator](/tools/missed-call-cost-calculator.html). It takes those two or three numbers and shows the yearly figure in words and in a table. This page works the same sum by hand so you can check it.

## Worked examples that add up

### One. One missed call a week at £250 where every caller would have booked

Yearly is 1 times £250 times 52 equals £13,000

Check: £250 times 52 equals £13,000

One call that would have been a £250 job each week for a year is £13,000 of work not won.

### Two. Three missed calls a week at £250

Yearly is 3 times £250 times 52 equals £39,000

Check: £250 times 3 equals £750 a week. £750 times 52 equals £39,000

Three calls that never came back is just over £100 a day across the working year that did not reach you.

### Three. One missed call a week at £500

Yearly is 1 times £500 times 52 equals £26,000

Check: £500 times 52 equals £26,000

One larger job a week at £500 is twice the figure at £250. The mix of small and large jobs in those calls matters more than the count.

### Four. Three at £500

Yearly is 3 times £500 times 52 equals £78,000

Check: £1,500 a week times 52 equals £78,000

Where your average job is larger the missed value rises in step.

### Five. The same counts at a 50 percent conversion rate

Not every caller becomes a customer. The conversion rate is the share that would have booked.

At 50 percent yearly is half the figures above.

* 1 a week at £250 times 52 times 0.5 equals £6,500
* 3 a week at £250 times 52 times 0.5 equals £19,500
* 1 a week at £500 times 52 times 0.5 equals £13,000
* 3 a week at £500 times 52 times 0.5 equals £39,000

Check: £39,000 times 0.5 equals £19,500. £78,000 times 0.5 equals £39,000

If you win half of what you quote for a conversion of 50 percent is closer to reality than 100 percent. If you know what share of enquiries become jobs use that figure as the rate.

### Six. A mixed year

Your calls are not all one price. Work two totals where the mix has a clear split.

Example. In a year you miss on average 2 small jobs worth £150 and 1 large job worth £500 each week.

Small: 2 times £150 times 52 equals £15,600

Large: 1 times £500 times 52 equals £26,000

Total: £15,600 plus £26,000 equals £41,600

Check: £15,600 plus £26,000 equals £41,600

At 50 percent conversion that year is £20,800

That is the cost of a typical week repeated. If you close for four weeks use 48 rather than 52 for a tighter figure.

At 50 percent halve each cell. So 3 a week at £250 is £19,500.

## What this is and what it is not

This is a price on lost chances using numbers you type. It is not a claim from us about your business. It is not a promise that calls exist at a set rate. It is not an expense you can claim against tax and it does not file anything with HMRC.

VAT is outside the sum. The job value you type can be net of VAT where you are VAT registered or gross where you are not. Keep the choice the same across the year.

UK VAT has three rates. 20 percent standard and 5 percent reduced and 0 percent zero rated. Read those rates on gov.uk on 24 October 2026. You must register when taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months since 1 April 2024. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

What helps besides counting is picking up faster and sending a reply where you cannot pick up and sending a quote the same day. Those three move the yearly figure more than any change to the price you set in the sum.

The app does not answer your phone. That line matters because no app should claim to be you on a call. When a call is missed the caller leaves a message that is written out and sorted and sent to you. The app never pretends to be the trader. That is how the missed calls feature is described on the site.
