# VAT threshold guide

> **Key facts**
>
> * You must register for VAT when taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months. That threshold has applied since 1 April 2024. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * The deregistration threshold is £88,000. You may apply to deregister when taxable turnover falls below that figure. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * UK VAT has three rates. 20 percent standard and 5 percent reduced and 0 percent zero rated. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.
> * You must register within 30 days of the end of the month in which you went over the threshold. Your effective date is the first day of the second month after you went over. Read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

<p class="tertiary">24 October 2026 · 7 min read · VAT</p>

VAT registration turns on one number. Taxable turnover over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months means you must register. This post sets out the test and how HMRC counts it and what to do if you go over.

All figures and dates in this post were read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026. The current threshold and the date it changed were checked on that date.

## The number to hold in your head

You must register for VAT if taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in any rolling 12 month period. Not a calendar year and not a tax year. A rolling 12 months that moves as each month passes.

That £90,000 threshold has applied since 1 April 2024. It rose from £85,000 on that date. That rise was announced at Spring Budget 2024. Read both dates on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

There is a second threshold. You may apply to deregister when taxable turnover falls below £88,000. That figure also changed on 1 April 2024. Read both thresholds on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

UK VAT has three rates. 20 percent standard and 5 percent reduced and 0 percent zero rated. Read those rates on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

## What counts as taxable turnover

Taxable turnover is the total value of everything you sell that is not VAT exempt or outside the scope of VAT.

It includes standard rated and reduced rated and zero rated sales. It also includes goods you hired or loaned to customers and business goods you used for personal use and goods you bartered or gave as gifts and some building work you did for your own business over £100,000. Read that list on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

It does not include VAT exempt sales and sales outside the scope of VAT. Check exempt versus zero rated where your work sits. Zero rated is taxable but at 0 percent. Exempt is not.

## The two tests

HMRC has two tests. If either is met you must register.

### Test one. You went over in the last 12 months

Add the last 12 months of taxable turnover at the end of each month. If that total is over £90,000 you must register. You have 30 days from the end of that month to apply. Your effective date is the first day of the second month after you went over.

Example. On 15 July your rolling 12 months reaches £100,000. That is the first time it has gone over £90,000. You must register by 30 August. Your effective date is 1 September.

That example is the one HMRC gives. It was read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

### Test two. You will go over in the next 30 days

If you realise your taxable turnover will go over £90,000 in the next 30 days you must register by the end of that 30 day period. Your effective date is the date you realised rather than the date turnover went over.

Example. On 1 May you sign a £100,000 deal payable at the end of May. You must apply by 30 May. Your effective date is 1 May.

That example was read on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

## What “over £90,000” means in practice

Check at month end. Not at year end.

Keep a simple rolling total. Each month add that month and drop the month from 13 months ago. Compare to £90,000.

| Month | This month | Rolling 12 months |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January | £6,800 | £86,400 |
| February | £9,200 | £88,900 |
| March | £7,100 | £90,300 |
| April | £5,900 | £89,400 but already over in March |

March is the trigger. Rolling total went over £90,000 to £90,300. You must register by 30 April. Effective date is 1 May.

Check: £86,400 plus £9,200 equals £95,600 at the end of February then adjusted for the dropped month gives £88,900 as in the table. The table is illustrative of the method rather than a single job.

If you think you have gone over temporarily you can ask HMRC for an exception. HMRC will consider it and write to confirm. If they refuse they will register you. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

If you only sell VAT exempt goods or services you do not have to register on turnover alone. If you are based in Northern Ireland and buy goods for more than £90,000 from EU VAT registered suppliers special rules apply. Read those on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

## How VAT looks on the job

Once you are registered you must charge VAT where it applies and show it as a separate line.

Example where VAT is at 20 percent. Price before VAT £1,200.00. VAT £240.00. Total £1,440.00.

Check: £1,200.00 plus £240.00 equals £1,440.00

To remove VAT from a VAT inclusive price divide by 1.20. A price of £1,440.00 divided by 1.20 equals £1,200.00. VAT is £240.00.

Check: £1,200.00 times 20 percent equals £240.00

Do not take 20 percent off a VAT inclusive price. That gives the wrong figure. Use divide by 1.20 for 20 percent. The VAT and remove VAT calculators show the method and the tables.

## Voluntary registration

You can register even when turnover is under £90,000. That is voluntary registration.

It lets you reclaim VAT on business purchases where VAT was charged. It also adds VAT to your sales so customers who are not VAT registered pay more. Where your customers are mainly householders it may not help. Where they are mainly businesses that can reclaim VAT it can be neutral to them and useful to you. Take advice on that choice where it matters.

## If you register late

If you register late you must pay VAT on sales from the date you should have been registered. You may also face a penalty depending on how much you owe and how late you are. Read that on gov.uk on 24 October 2026.

## What to do next

Keep the rolling 12 month total up to date. Set a reminder at month end.

If you are near £88,000 to £90,000 check the next 30 days. A signed contract that will push you over £90,000 within that window triggers test two early.

If you go over apply within 30 days. Note your effective date and start charging VAT from that date. Keep sales and purchase records so the return is correct.
