# YellowTradie against a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet is a good tool and most solo traders start on one. It is free if you already have Excel or Google Sheets or Numbers. It does not go down. You can shape it exactly how you want. That is a real strength and this comparison starts there.

Our verdict is that a spreadsheet is the best way to keep a simple list, and YellowTradie is the better fit when you want the quote to write itself and the job to carry its own record. Keep the sheet if the list is all you need. Move when you want the phone to do the next step for you.

Prices checked on 24 October 2026. YellowTradie checked on its own pricing page. A spreadsheet has no price to check because you already own the app that opens it.

## When YellowTradie is the better fit

Choose YellowTradie if you want:

- to speak the job on site and have the quote fill itself line by line from your own words
- the customer to open a link and approve the quote, with the answer back on your phone
- one place for the job that holds the quote and the invoice and the costs and the photos and the hours
- a reminder when an invoice is past due that asks for the balance and already knows your bank details
- to see what was actually paid, what is still owed, and what to set aside for tax without building a formula

## What a spreadsheet does better

A spreadsheet is free to run and you already know how it looks. You can change a column in a second and copy a sheet for the next year without asking anyone. It will not close an account or ask for a new login. If you have a simple set of jobs and you are happy to build the formulas, a sheet can be all you need for a year.

It also has no rules about what a document must look like. You can put any logo or any note anywhere on the page. The app has to keep the invoice in a set shape so the customer link and the PDF match and the bank block stays in the right place.

The price difference is real too. A spreadsheet has no monthly bill. YellowTradie is £19.99 a month, and it is an extra bill to carry if the list is all you use.

## Questions

### Is a spreadsheet really free

In the sense this table checks, yes. If you have Excel or Google Sheets or Numbers, there is no extra charge per month to keep a sheet. Google Sheets is free with a Google account. Your phone and your laptop are costs you already have. YellowTradie checked on 24 October 2026 is £19.99 a month with 14 days free.

### Can I keep my sheet if I try YellowTradie

Yes. Nothing in the app deletes a file. Keep the sheet as your backup while you run the first few jobs in the app. Export a sheet from the app for the accountant when you need a copy outside the phone.

### Will my sheet chase a late customer

Not on its own. You will see that a due date has passed if you built a column for it. You will still write the message and fill in the bank details and the amount still owed. In YellowTradie the chase card appears on Home with that message already filled in, and you choose when to send.

### What if I have no signal on site

Both work. A sheet that is saved to the device opens with no signal. Google Sheets needs you to turn on offline access before you need it. In YellowTradie the jobs and quotes are on the phone, so you can type a quote with no signal. A spoken phrase needs a signal to be turned into lines, but the typed form does not.

### Does VAT work in a spreadsheet

You can make it work if you add the rate and the formula. You also have to keep the reverse charge right, for example removing VAT by dividing by 1.20. In the app the VAT rate is set once in Settings and the totals on quotes and invoices follow it.

### What does a spreadsheet not do that the app does

A sheet does not give the customer a link to answer on. It does not keep photos and costs tied to the job without extra linking. It does not remind you when a bill is late. It does not keep a payment log that can take a part payment and show the ageing. It does not answer a question from your own records.
